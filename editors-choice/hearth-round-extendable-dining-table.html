A Dining Table That Seats More Than It Looks

Crate & Barrel · Hearth 49"–71" Oak Round Extendable Dining Table

Product

Why We Like It

A 48-inch round is the most forgiving dining shape for a small room: no corners to walk around, everyone can see everyone, and it takes up noticeably less visual space than a rectangle of the same seating. Hearth adds the trick that makes it practical, a stored leaf that turns it into a 70-inch oval for six.

The design is quiet in the right way. A thick, arc-edged top and rounded plank legs give it presence without ornament, and the legs are angled outward so knees clear them, which matters on a round table more than people expect.

Oak with a wire-brushed finish shows grain and hides the small marks a dining table collects.

Design Notes

  • Allow 36 inches of clearance around the round footprint, 42 on the side people pass through.
  • With the leaf in, the oval needs roughly 11 feet of room length to seat six comfortably.
  • Pairs well with armless chairs; check that chair arms clear the recessed apron if you prefer armchairs.
  • A 6-by-9 rug fits under the round; go to 8-by-10 if you extend it often.

What to Consider

The top is oak veneer over engineered wood rather than solid planks, which keeps it stable and affordable but limits deep refinishing. It is a good table for fifteen years, not necessarily for fifty.

This is a newer design with few reviews at the time of checking, so long-term reports are thin.

Why It Is in Editor's Choice

Round-to-oval flexibility + honest proportions + a finish that wears well + fair price

Styling Pairing

Pair with:

  • Four light spindle or bentwood chairs
  • One pendant centered over the table, bottom 30 to 36 inches above the top
  • A low ceramic bowl rather than a tall centerpiece
  • A round or oval rug sized to the extended table
