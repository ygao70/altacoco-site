A Solid-Wood Dining Table Built to Last

Crate & Barrel · Terra 90" Natural White Oak Solid Wood Dining Table

Product

Why We Like It

This is what a solid-wood table should be: inch-and-a-half planks of white oak, hand-finished, with legs that pass through the top so the end grain shows on the surface. There is no apron to hide the joinery and nothing to peel or delaminate. In thirty years it can be sanded and oiled and look new.

Ninety inches seats eight without anyone sharing a corner, and the 37-inch width is generous enough for serving dishes down the center.

The ivory ceruse finish sits in the grain and keeps the oak pale and Scandinavian rather than orange, which is the finish most people are looking for right now and few solid tables offer.

Design Notes

  • Needs a room at least 13 feet long to allow 36-inch clearance at both ends.
  • Solid oak moves with the seasons; a humidifier in a dry winter house keeps joints tight.
  • Knots, checking, and color variation are expected and are the point.
  • Bench on one side and chairs on the other keeps the heavy table from feeling heavier.

What to Consider

It weighs over 200 pounds and does not come apart; plan the delivery path.

The natural finish is not a thick film; use trivets and wipe spills. It will develop a patina, which is either the appeal or the drawback depending on the household.

Why It Is in Editor's Choice

Solid FSC white oak + honest joinery + refinishable for life + a pale finish that suits modern rooms

Styling Pairing

Pair with:

  • Six to eight light-framed chairs in oak or black
  • A linear pendant or two matching ceramic pendants
  • A wool runner on the table rather than a full cloth
  • A credenza in the same white oak
